# Critique of PRD draft 0.9, and a blind alternative plan compared with plan 0.5

Date: 2026-11-27. Inputs: `design/PRD.md` (0.9), `design/greenfield_architecture_plan.md`
(0.5), `design/specs/2026-10-31-phase0-m1-design.md`, the M1 S2 plan, the review of plan 0.4,
and the code on this branch. The alternative plan,
`design/research/2026-11-27-alternative-architecture-blind.md`, was written by an agent that
read only the PRD, AGENTS.md and `design/literature/`. Status: a review for the owner. Nothing
here is decided.

## 1. Verdict

**The science in the PRD is right and should stay as it is. What is wrong is the weight of
the PRD: too many requirements carry equal priority, it doubles as a decision log, and
nothing in it says what gets cut when time runs short.** The architecture is not the
problem. Three independent drafts have now reached the same skeleton: plan 0.5, the minimal
draft in the plan 0.4 review (§5), and the blind draft from today. The risk to the §1.2 success
criteria is sequencing and process weight. The requirements that drive adoption are SPI,
adjusted angles and a course that installs. They sit behind a long M1.

Read together: agents write code quickly, and the owner's attention is the slow part. The
PRD's own risk table (§11) names maintainer capacity, but it treats that capacity as coding
time.

## 3. What the PRD gets right (keep)

- **Acceptance is numeric wherever a number exists:** the 56 exclusions (FR-1.5), the SPI
  paper's tables (journey C), the `sn` fixtures (FR-3.4). This is the PRD's best feature.
- **The out-of-scope list is honest:** Methods B and C, SEM fitting, report generation,
  multichannel audio, the GUI itself.
- **The predictor interface is fixed and the model choice deferred** (journey D, FR-5.3).
  This is the right split under uncertainty.
- **Profiles are named after the sources they follow** (FR-2.9), so a consultant can say
  which source a number complies with.
- **One per-response status column explains every exclusion** (FR-1.5). It works for the
  science and for a GUI.

## 4. Where the PRD is weak

### 4.1 Priority does not tell requirements apart

With about 73 Musts, "Must" means only "in this milestone". Nothing marks the 1.0 cut line:
what has to exist for the course and for consultants to use SPI, and what can follow in an
alpha. Plan 0.4 had the same flaw, and its review fixed it in the plan (commit, rule, defer).
The PRD never got that treatment.

### 4.2 It is a requirements document and a decision log at once

§10 runs to about 130 lines of history (10.1, 10.2, 10.3, 10.4, 10.5, 10.5a, 10.5b, 10.6). Many
requirements carry dated `[agreed …]` tags and process notes. A reader cannot tell the
current requirement from the story of how it was reached. Plan 0.5 §9 is already the
decision register, so the PRD should point to it rather than repeat it.

### 4.3 Implementation leaks into requirements

Each of these is architecture written as a requirement, so changing it needs a PRD edit:

- FR-14.2: "an open columnar format with a JSON record";
- FR-12.1: Quarto `.qmd`;
- FR-3.2, and §10.5's note on it: "at the drawing layer";
- FR-14.4: the directory contents;
- NFR-13: the details of the `logging` handler;
- FR-8.3: "the conventions of the underlying plotting library".

State the user need instead (inspectable, open, reopenable; tutorials executed in CI;
silenceable output) and leave the mechanism to the plan.

### 4.4 Requirements in tension

- **NFR-9 against FR-11.1, FR-14.2 and FR-6.3.** "No bespoke frameworks" sits beside
  provenance on every result, content identity and full serialisation. Together those are a
  bespoke framework, and S1 shows the cost: 29% of its source is infrastructure. The PRD
  should name this as the one framework it accepts, and bound it ("one envelope, one hashing
  helper, nothing more until the GUI").
- **FR-2.0 against the "second implementation" rule.** FR-2.0 says every analysis, plot and
  score works for any circumplex "without special-casing". But no non-outdoor instrument is
  tested in M1 or M2, so the requirement cannot be verified. Either ship the Torresin indoor
  circumplex as a test fixture, or reword FR-2.0 as a design rule ("positions, not words")
  with acceptance on the outdoor instance.
- **FR-14.5 (a CLI for journey A at M1) against the personas.** P5 works in notebooks. P2 and
  P3 get direct use with the GUI. No persona needs a CLI before the GUI. Yet it is a whole M1
  slice (S8).
- **NFR-12 against the pace of agent work.** The owner reviews every change that affects
  scientific results. That is right, but nothing sizes it. With agent throughput this high,
  owner review is the actual critical path, and it is absent from §11.

### 4.5 Musts that cannot be tested

Already listed in §10.6 and still unanswered:

- FR-1.3a "easy for a non-programmer";
- FR-8.3 "publication-quality";
- FR-12.1 and journey K "under an hour";
- journey L "under a day".

Each needs an operational test (a timed run with one named kind of user, a checklist) or a
lower priority. Otherwise "done" becomes a judgement call made at release.

### 4.6 Success has no numbers

All five §1.2 criteria are adoption trends. None says when 1.0 is good enough. One or two
numbers would discipline scope, for example:

- the share of a course cohort that reaches the first plot by the documented path;
- SPI used in at least N external projects or papers within a year.

### 4.7 Risks §11 leaves out

- **Owner bandwidth for inputs and review.** Not the same as coding capacity (§4.4).
- **The course calendar.** The PRD gives no date anywhere, so it cannot say whether M2 is
  late.
- **Dependence on one chapter.** The SATP v1.5 angles and tiers come from notes on an
  unpublished chapter with known proof inconsistencies (spec §10). M1's headline default
  rests on it.
- **Process overhead.** Design text runs at 1.6 times the code. Each slice plan draws dozens
  of review findings. That cost recurs every slice.

## 5. The blind alternative plan against plan 0.5

### 5.1 Where it agrees: the skeleton holds

The blind draft chose much the same structure as plan 0.5:

- a functional core over numpy;
- frozen pydantic settings per operation, saved as TOML;
- profiles as versioned bundles of settings;
- versioned data files that are never edited;
- geometry kept separate from drawing;
- `scipy.stats.gaussian_kde`, matched to seaborn's settings;
- a project directory of parquet tables with JSON sidecars;
- standard entry points for plugins;
- import-linter layering;
- click for the CLI;
- one per-response status.

This is the third independent draft to reach the same skeleton. Treat the shape as settled.

### 5.2 Where it differs, and which is better

| Topic | Blind draft | Plan 0.5 | Assessment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dataframes | pandas only at the edges, polars converted on the way in | narwhals at the boundary (owner decision #2) | **Worth reopening.** `_frames.py` (509 lines) is S1's largest module, with 353 lines of tests. The blind draft's argument, that the cost falls on every contributor and buys only native polars output, now has cost figures behind it. This is the owner's decision. |
| Excluded responses | Left out of the coordinates table | Rows kept, with NaN | **Plan 0.5.** It keeps the row count fixed and keeps joins trivial. |
| Status | An append-only log (response, stage, reason), with the status column derived from it | `included`, `reasons`, `flags` | **Plan 0.5**, slightly. `reasons` already keeps every cause. Recording which stage excluded a response adds little. |
| Warnings | Captured into each result's provenance, then re-shown by plots and exports | Shared label helpers in `plot` | **Blind draft's idea, adopt at M4.** It meets FR-5.9's "labelled in every table and plot" in one place. Note it in the M4 spec, not now. |
| Built-in features | Registered through the same entry points as plugins | Plugin groups created with their first member | **Plan 0.5.** The blind draft breaks the plan's rule 8 ("an abstraction comes with its second implementation"). |
| SPI settings | The KS variant, direction combination, tie rule and truncation are all parameters from day one | Implement the paper's combination only, and record it | **Plan 0.5.** A smaller surface; recording the method is what FR-6.1 asks for. |
| Predictor protocol | Frozen at M2 with a dummy model | Shape fixed, code at M4 | **Plan 0.5** (rule 8). |
| Result directories | Keyed by a hash of the inputs, which gives caching for free | Runs keyed by name | Either works. Leave it until a user needs caching. |
| Contours | contourpy, named | Left to the spec | Neutral. contourpy is what matplotlib itself uses, so it is a reasonable default for the S5 spec. |
| M1 size | 5 slices | 12 slices (S0 to S11) | **The blind draft's pressure is right, but its cuts are not.** It keeps the CLI and `suggest_mapping` in M1. Its cut list (renormalisation, ipsatisation, clustering, MOSAIQ, multi-seed SPI, report content, nightly reproductions) is mostly deferred in plan 0.5 already. |

The blind draft caught one specification point that plan 0.5 and the spec already handle:
the canonical formula equals the 2019 one only with 45° angles on a 1..5 scale. The spec
refuses `iso2019` on 1..7.

### 5.3 Net

The alternative plan does not beat plan 0.5. Do not replace the plan. Take three things from
it: reopen the narwhals decision with the S1 cost figures, keep warnings-in-provenance for
the M4 spec, and accept its pressure to make M1 shorter.

## 6. Recommendations, ranked

1. **Request the M2 owner inputs now, not when M2 is planned** (PRD §10.3): the KS variant
   and how its directions combine, the tie rule and truncation, the 2024 targets and sample
   size, the R `sn` run, the Example 6 data. They are the only M2 work agents cannot do. At
   the current pace, waiting for M2 planning puts them on the critical path.
2. **Draw a 1.0 cut line in the PRD.** Tag each Must as "1.0" (needed for M1 and M2 to serve
   the course and SPI users) or "by its milestone". Mark the M3 to M5 requirements as
   provisional, to be re-confirmed when each milestone is planned. This is the same
   treatment the plan 0.4 review gave the plan.
3. **Shorten M1.** Move the journey A CLI (FR-14.5, spec S8) to after M2, and move
   `suggest_mapping` and Qualtrics detection (FR-1.3a, S7) to Should. Neither serves a
   persona before the GUI. The PRD changes are FR-14.5's timing and FR-1.3a's priority.
4. **Add the missing risks to §11:** owner review and input bandwidth, the course calendar
   (with an actual target date), and dependence on the chapter.
5. **Make §1.2 measurable** with one or two numbers (§4.6).
6. **Split the PRD from its history.** Move §10.1 to §10.5b into a decisions log, or point
   to plan §9. Remove the implementation leaks of §4.3. The PRD should get shorter and stop
   changing every time a slice is specified.
7. **Settle the four untestable Musts** (§4.5) when §10.6 is next answered.
8. **Reopen owner decision #2 (narwhals)** with the S1 figures (§5.2). Decide it before S4
   adds pyarrow and the workspace, because each slice built on the boundary helper raises
   the cost of switching.
9. **Resolve the FR-2.0 tension** (§4.4): ship an indoor test circumplex, or reword FR-2.0
   as a design rule.

## 7. What would change this verdict

- **If the course date is far off and the owner has time for review,** sequencing matters
  less. Recommendations 1 and 3 weaken; 2 and 6 still hold.
- **If a polars user group exists among the course or research users,** recommendation 8
  falls away.
- **If the M2 inputs have already been gathered outside the repo,** recommendation 1 is done.
  It only needs recording in PRD §10.3.
